Day 2: Routers and Switches

Task 1: Labelled Diagram of a Router and Network Switch

A. Home Wi-Fi Router

A home Wi-Fi router connects devices to the internet and allows devices to communicate with each other.

Important Parts:

* WAN Port: Connects the router to the modem or internet connection.
* LAN Ports: Connect computers and other wired devices.
* Power Port: Supplies power to the router.
* Status LEDs: Indicate power, internet connectivity, Wi-Fi activity, and LAN connections.
* Antennas: Help provide wireless connectivity.

Diagram: Insert a labelled router diagram showing the WAN port, LAN ports, power port, antennas, and status LEDs.

B. Network Switch

A network switch connects multiple wired devices within a local area network (LAN).

Important Parts:

* Ethernet Ports: Connect computers and other network devices.
* Power Port: Supplies power to the switch.
* Status LEDs: Show link status and network activity for each port.
* Console Port: Available on some managed switches for configuration.

Task 2: Three Differences Between Routers and Switches

Feature	Router	Switch
Main Function	Connects different networks	Connects devices within a LAN
Address Used	Uses IP addresses to route packets	Uses MAC addresses to forward Ethernet frames
Network Traffic	Routes traffic between networks	Forwards traffic within a local network

Task 3: Identify Ports and LED Indicators

A. Router

* Power Port: Usually located at the back of the router; connects to the power adapter.
* WAN Port: Connects to the modem or internet service provider’s equipment.
* Ethernet LAN Ports: Connect wired devices such as computers and switches.
* Status LEDs: Usually located on the front; indicate power, internet, Wi-Fi, and Ethernet activity.

B. Network Switch

* Power Port: Connects the switch to its power supply.
* Ethernet Ports: Connect computers, printers, routers, and other network devices.
* Status LEDs: Usually beside or above the Ethernet ports; indicate link, speed, and activity.
* Console Port: Present on some managed switches for administration and configuration.

Task 4: Gaming Café Network Scenario

Imagine setting up a small gaming café with eight gaming PCs for IPL streaming and multiplayer gaming.

First, I would connect the internet service provider (ISP) connection to the modem or ONT, if required. Then I would connect the modem or ONT to the WAN port of the router.

Next, I would connect one LAN port of the router to an Ethernet port on a network switch. I would connect all eight gaming PCs to the switch using Ethernet cables.

Reasons for my choices:

1. The router connects the café’s local network to the internet and routes traffic between networks.
2. The switch provides enough Ethernet ports to connect all eight gaming PCs.
3. Wired Ethernet connections offer stable connectivity and generally lower latency for multiplayer gaming.
4. A suitable internet plan and router help support streaming and gaming traffic.

Conclusion: The router provides internet connectivity, while the switch connects all eight gaming PCs to the local network.
